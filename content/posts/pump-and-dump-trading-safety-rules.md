## Anatomy of an Artificial Market Rally

Every pump begins with a phase of stealth accumulation. Organizers select a low-liquidity, small-cap asset where even a relatively small volume of buy orders can trigger a significant price move. At this stage, the chart may look stationary, but signs of abnormal activity or gradual absorption of supply begin to appear in the order book. Once the position is built, the marketing machine kicks in: social media, private messenger groups, and influencers start actively promoting a unique opportunity or insider information. The price spikes parabolically, attracting algorithmic bots and traders susceptible to FOMO. At this point, the organizers are already preparing to dump on the unsuspecting crowd.

## Indicators of Imminent Manipulation

Recognizing a pump at an early stage is a critical skill. The first red flag is a sharp spike in trading volume in the absence of significant fundamental news. If an asset price climbs 50-100% in a matter of hours without official partnerships or tech updates, it is a classic sign of manipulation. Pay attention to buy walls in the order book: manipulators often place large buy orders below the current price to create the illusion of strong support, but pull them the moment the actual sell-off begins. Abnormal social media activity, where identical accounts simultaneously force the same ticker to create artificial hype, is another key indicator.

## Technical Analysis in Turbulent Conditions

Traditional tools function specifically during pumps. The RSI on short timeframes can remain in the extreme overbought zone for hours, which in a normal market signals a reversal, but in a pump scenario, it merely confirms the strength of the manipulative impulse. Traders should focus on vertical volume and the closing dynamics of five-minute candles. The appearance of long upper wicks indicates that large holders have begun taking profits. It is important to monitor Fibonacci levels: corrections often begin after reaching the 1.618 or 2.618 extension levels, but relying solely on these without confirmation from the order flow is extremely dangerous.

## Risk Management Strategy for Pumps

The golden rule of safety is to never enter a trade on a vertical spike when the price has already moved up more than 30-40%. If you decide to participate in a volatile move, your position size should not exceed 1-2% of your total trading capital. These are high-risk assets where a total loss of funds is possible. Stop-losses are mandatory in such scenarios, but one must account for slippage: during a sharp dump, a market order may execute much worse than the expected price due to a lack of liquidity.
