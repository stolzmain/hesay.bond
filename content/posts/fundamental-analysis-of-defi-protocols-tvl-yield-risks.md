## TVL as a Core Liquidity Indicator

The Total Value Locked (TVL) metric is traditionally considered the primary measure of a DeFi protocol's success. It reflects the total value of assets deposited into the platform's smart contracts. However, an experienced analyst knows that nominal TVL can be misleading. It is crucial to evaluate not only the total sum but also the composition of assets. A protocol whose TVL consists of 80 percent of its own volatile token is far less robust than a project operating with ETH, BTC, or stablecoins. Furthermore, it is necessary to consider the Market Cap to TVL (MCap/TVL) ratio. If this metric significantly exceeds one, the project may be overvalued. The true value of TVL lies in its stickiness: how quickly capital will flee the platform if yields drop. Quality analysis implies tracking TVL dynamics during periods of market turbulence.

## Capital Efficiency and Fee Volume

TVL on its own generates no profit if it is not put to work. Analyzing Protocol Revenue becomes a key stage of the assessment. Here, one must distinguish between total fees paid by users and net income distributed to token holders or directed to the treasury. High turnover on a decentralized exchange (DEX) or high loan volume in a lending protocol with a relatively small TVL indicates high capital efficiency. This means the protocol is in demand by the market as a financial tool, not just as a storage vault. Comparing a protocol's revenue with its operating expenses helps determine whether the business model is self-sustaining or subsidized through the emission of governance tokens.

## Real Yield vs. Token Inflation

In the DeFi space, there is a concept called Real Yield—income paid out in hard-asset denominations (e.g., ETH or stablecoins) generated from the protocol's actual economic activity. The opposite is yield based on the issuance of new native tokens. If a 50 percent APY is provided solely by the inflation of a token that lacks burn mechanisms or utility, the value of that asset will inevitably trend toward zero. An analyst must compare the inflation rate with actual cash flows. A project is considered sustainable when liquidity provider yields are backed by user fees rather than a marketing budget funded by a money printer.

## Technological and Financial Risks

Fundamental analysis is impossible without assessing risks, which in DeFi fall into three categories: smart contract risks, oracle risks, and economic risks. Having audits from top-tier firms like OpenZeppelin or Trail of Bits is the mandatory minimum, but it is not a guarantee of safety. It is vital to examine the history of exploits and how the team responded to incidents. Oracle risks are associated with potential price manipulation in low-liquidity markets, which can lead to unjustified liquidations.
