## Specifics and Structure of Bitcoin Contracts

The standard Bitcoin futures contract on the CME has a size of 5 BTC, making it a tool primarily for large market participants. There is also a micro contract (MBT), equivalent to 0.1 BTC, which is accessible to retail traders with smaller capital. A critical feature is that contracts are cash-settled based on the CME CF Bitcoin Reference Rate. This means that at expiration, the trader receives the price difference in US dollars rather than the cryptocurrency itself. Each tick (minimum price increment) for a standard contract is 5 points, equivalent to 25 dollars. For successful trading, it is necessary to account for the expiration date, as liquidity migrates from the current front-month to the next several days before the contract closes.

## Margin Requirements and Leverage

Unlike unregulated crypto exchanges where leverage can reach 1:100, the CME maintains a conservative policy. Margin requirements are set by the clearinghouse and typically range from 30% to 50% of the contract value. This significantly reduces the risk of sudden liquidations due to short-term market noise, but requires the trader to have a substantial deposit. Operations are conducted through licensed Futures Commission Merchants (FCMs), which provide an additional layer of fund security. Traders must clearly distinguish between Initial Margin, required to open a position, and Maintenance Margin, which must be maintained to keep a trade open.

## The Phenomenon of Price Gaps

One of the most popular strategies on the CME is gap trading. Because the exchange closes on weekends and holidays, while the Bitcoin spot market operates 24/7, price gaps often emerge on Monday mornings between Friday's closing price and the opening of the new session. A statistically significant portion of these gaps are filled in the near term. Professional analysts use these zones as magnet levels for price. However, it is important to understand that gap filling is not a guaranteed event, and using this strategy without accounting for the overall trend and market context can lead to losses. CME gaps serve as an excellent indicator of market sentiment and institutional imbalance.

## Using COT Reports for Analysis

Trading on the CME provides a unique advantage: access to weekly Commitment of Traders (COT) reports. This data allows you to see how positions are distributed among various participant categories, including asset managers, dealers, and small traders. By analyzing the net positions of large players, one can identify accumulation or distribution phases of the asset. If institutional players are consistently increasing their longs, it serves as strong confirmation of a long-term bullish trend. Comparing Open Interest with price dynamics allows you to identify the inflow of fresh capital into the market, which is critical for confirming the validity of breakouts from key support and resistance levels.
