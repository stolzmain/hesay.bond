## The FOMO Phenomenon and Price Euphoria

When an asset begins to skyrocket, the social space becomes saturated with stories of overnight riches. At this moment, FOMO (Fear Of Missing Out) kicks in. Psychologically, it is extremely difficult for a person to remain on the sidelines when everyone else appears to be making money. At the peak of a trend, crowd confidence reaches its maximum, and the fear of risk is dulled. The investor sees green candles on the chart and perceives them as a guarantee of further growth. In a biological sense, this triggers a dopamine release: the anticipation of easy gains clouds critical thinking. As a result, buys are executed at the point of maximum market overheating, just as large players (smart money) begin to exit their positions, offloading their bags onto the influx of liquidity from retail traders.

## The Pain of Loss and Panic Selling

The opposite situation arises during a deep correction. Fear is a much more powerful emotion than greed. According to Kahneman and Tversky’s prospect theory, the sting of losing 1,000 dollars is felt twice as intensely as the joy of gaining the same amount. When the price drops, the investor first denies reality, then hopes for a bounce, and finally descends into panic. The point of capitulation arrives when the psychological pressure becomes unbearable. A person sells their assets at the absolute bottom not because it is rational, but to put an end to emotional pain. It is at this exact moment that professionals begin to accumulate, using the mass dumping of assets to build their portfolios at bargain prices.

## Brain Biology vs. Financial Success

Our instincts were formed in an environment where following the tribe was the key to survival. If everyone is running in one direction, it means there is either food or salvation from a predator. In trading, this mechanism of social proof works against us. The brain perceives a market chart as a direct threat or opportunity, activating the amygdala, the brain's fear center. This blocks the prefrontal cortex, which is responsible for logical planning and analysis. A trader acting on instinct turns into part of a biological mass moving in lockstep. To buy when others are fearful and sell when others are euphoric requires not just intelligence, but the willpower to suppress natural physiological responses.

## Social Proof and Market Noise

Modern information streams only amplify destructive crowd behavior. Social media and mainstream news act as resonators, echoing market sentiment. When news of a new Bitcoin record or a tech giant's stock surge hits the headlines, it is a surefire sign that the cycle is nearing its end. The crowd always learns about a trend last, when the potential for movement is already exhausted. Collective thinking strips the individual investor of objectivity. They stop analyzing fundamentals and begin to rely on the opinion of the majority, which, as history shows from the tulip mania to the dot-com bubble, is always wrong during critical trend reversals.
