## Cyclical Logic of Market Movement

Market processes are rarely linear. They evolve in waves, where each new cycle of volatility expansion or contraction repeats previous patterns at a new level. The spiral as a geometric figure perfectly describes this process: it combines circular motion (cyclicality) and progressive development (trend). The Spiral indicator brings this concept to the chart, helping the trader see which phase of the cycle an asset is currently in. Unlike standard oscillators that only capture overbought or oversold conditions, the spiral points to harmonic completion points of market phases, based on mathematical proportions akin to the golden ratio.

## Mathematical Foundation of the Spiral Indicator

The foundation of most versions of this indicator lies in Fibonacci sequences and Gann principles. Construction begins from a significant extremum—a point where a new trend originated or a sharp reversal occurred. From this center, arcs are built, the radius of which increases according to a mathematical progression. The core idea is that price and time are inextricably linked. When the price chart crosses the spiral line, a resonance occurs, which often leads to a halt in movement or a change in direction. This allows the trader to forecast not only where the price will go, but when it will happen, which is a critical edge in conditions of high volatility.

## Interaction of Price and Time Parameters

A key feature of the Spiral indicator is its ability to work with the squaring of price and time. In classical analysis, time is considered an independent variable that simply moves forward. In spiral analysis, time is viewed as a resource required to complete a specific price path. If the price moves too quickly, it encounters resistance from the outer boundary of the spiral ahead of schedule, signaling exhaustion of momentum. Conversely, a slow drift of the price toward a spiral arc may indicate the accumulation of strength for a powerful breakout. Thus, the indicator visualizes invisible boundaries that keep the price within a specific energy channel.

## Interpreting Signals on the Chart

Practical use of the spiral requires a trader to master working with non-linear levels. Points where the price intersects with the spiral arcs are potential reversal zones or areas of strong support. If the price consolidates along a spiral line, it indicates confirmation of the cycle's significance. Special attention should be paid to moments when the price moves beyond the last calculated arc—this often marks the start of a global trend or a shift in the market paradigm. Also effective is the method of overlaying several spirals from different extrema: their intersection zones (clusters) possess exceptional predictive power, pointing to critical trend-break points.

## Psychology of Market Expansion and Contraction

The effectiveness of the Spiral indicator is explained not only by mathematics but also by mass psychology. The market crowd acts cyclically: periods of euphoria are replaced by apathy, and fear by greed.
