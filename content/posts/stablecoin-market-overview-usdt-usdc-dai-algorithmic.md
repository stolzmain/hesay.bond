## USDT: Leadership Through Liquidity and Reach

Tether (USDT) remains the undisputed hegemon, commanding over 70% of the market share. Its dominance is driven by a network effect: USDT is integrated into almost every trading pair on centralized exchanges and is the gold standard for cross-border payments, particularly on the Tron and Ethereum networks. Despite years of criticism from U.S. regulators and questions regarding the quality of its reserves, Tether continues to post record-breaking profits by allocating funds into U.S. Treasury bills and gold. For a professional trader, USDT is first and foremost about liquidity. However, one must account for centralization risks: the issuer possesses the technical capability to freeze addresses, a feature actively utilized following law enforcement requests.

## USDC: Transparency and Regulatory Trust

USDC, issued by Circle, is positioned as a safe haven for institutional capital. Unlike its primary competitor, Circle adheres to a strategy of maximum transparency and strict compliance with U.S. legislation. USDC reserves are held at leading U.S. financial institutions, including BlackRock, and undergo regular independent audits. This makes the asset the preferred choice for large funds and DeFi protocols focused on Western markets. The turbulent period in the spring of 2023, when USDC briefly depegged due to issues at Silicon Valley Bank, served as a vital lesson: even high-quality reserves do not insulate against systemic banking risks. Nevertheless, USDC remains the benchmark for compliance.

## DAI: Decentralization and Real World Assets

DAI, issued by the MakerDAO protocol, represents a complex hybrid. Originally conceived as a fully decentralized asset backed by cryptocurrencies (ETH, WBTC), DAI has undergone significant evolution. Today, a substantial portion of its collateral consists of RWA (Real World Assets) such as treasury bills and corporate bonds. This has allowed MakerDAO to generate stable yields, though it has sparked debate regarding the loss of its "cypherpunk spirit." For users, DAI is attractive due to its protocol-level censorship resistance: its issuance is governed by smart contracts via an over-collateralization system. This makes it a crucial element of the DeFi ecosystem, despite having lower liquidity compared to centralized counterparts.

## Algorithmic Stablecoins and New Models

After the collapse of the Terra (UST) ecosystem in 2022, trust in algorithmic models was severely undermined. However, the industry is not standing still and is introducing new approaches. Modern projects, such as Frax Finance, utilize hybrid collateralization methods, combining backed assets with algorithmic stabilization. The "synthetic dollar" segment stands apart, with Ethena (USDe) being a prominent example. Instead of traditional collateral, it employs a delta-neutral strategy based on derivatives. Although such assets offer high yields, they carry specific risks related to negative funding rates and the complexity of strategy execution.
