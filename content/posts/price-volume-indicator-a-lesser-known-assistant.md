The Price Volume (PV) indicator is a tool that synthesizes these metrics, allowing you to see not just price action, but the energy behind every tick. While classic volume only shows the number of traded contracts, PV evaluates the quality of that volume relative to the distance covered. This makes it an indispensable assistant for identifying false breakouts and hidden asset accumulation phases by whales, whose actions often go unnoticed with a standard approach.

## Technical Nature and Calculation Logic

The indicator is based on a mathematical model that links percentage price change to total volume over a specific period. Unlike the On-Balance Volume (OBV) indicator, which simply sums up volume, Price Volume assigns a weight to each move. If the price rises on significant volume, the indicator shows a sharp spike, confirming an influx of liquidity. If the price moves up but volume falls or remains stagnant, PV signals a hollow move that is highly likely to end in a pullback. This tool allows a trader to distinguish inertial hype from a fundamentally justified trend by providing data on how efficiently capital is pushing the price in a chosen direction.

## Confirming the Validity of Trend Moves

The main value of Price Volume lies in filtering market noise during periods of high volatility. When there is a steady uptrend in the market, the PV curve should synchronously update local highs along with the price chart. This is an ideal scenario indicating healthy buyer interest. If you see the price continuing to rise, creating new peaks, while the indicator begins to stagnate or decline, this is a classic sign of exhaustion. Such a situation suggests that large holders are beginning to take profits, unloading their positions onto late retail traders. Using PV helps you exit a trade in time without waiting for a trend reversal and losing accumulated profits.

## Finding Reversals Through Hidden Divergences

Divergence between price and PV is one of the most powerful leading signals in technical analysis. Unlike oscillators like RSI or Stochastic, which can stay in overbought or oversold zones for a long time during a strong trend, PV produces fewer false signals because it is directly tied to cash flow. A bullish divergence occurs when the price forms a new low, but the Price Volume indicator shows a higher local low. This means selling is happening with low interest and bearish pressure is drying up. For a professional analyst, this is a surefire sign that the market has found a bottom and is preparing for an aggressive recovery phase, allowing you to enter a position at the very beginning of a new impulse.

## Effective Filtering of False Breakouts

One of the most difficult tasks for a trader is working with support and resistance levels. Price Volume acts as a lie detector here. When breaking an important level, the price often makes a sharp jerk. If this move is accompanied by explosive PV growth, the breakout is considered valid because it is backed by real capital. If the breakout occurs in a thin market with a low indicator value, there is a high probability of a bull trap or a bear trap forming.
