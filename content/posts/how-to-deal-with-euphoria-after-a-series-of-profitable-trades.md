## The danger of a hormonal surge

During moments of triumph, the body experiences a massive dopamine release. This neurotransmitter is responsible for feelings of reward, but it has a side effect: it reduces the ability to objectively assess risks. A trader in a state of euphoria stops seeing threats. They feel as though they have read the market, caught the wave, and now possess a magical intuition. In reality, at this moment, the prefrontal cortex, which is responsible for logic and planning, gives way to the emotional centers. In this state, it is easy to violate trading system rules, enter a trade without a clear signal, or ignore a stop-loss, assuming the price will definitely turn around, just as it did the previous five times.

## The illusion of control and overconfidence

A series of wins creates a dangerous illusion of control over chaotic market processes. A professional analyst understands that any profitable trade is merely the result of realizing a statistical edge at a specific moment in time, rather than a consequence of their own genius. A novice, however, begins to attribute market noise to their own outstanding abilities. Overconfidence leads to increasing leverage and position sizing beyond acceptable limits. The logic of I am on a roll, I need to squeeze out the maximum is the shortest route to a margin call. As soon as the market changes its character—which it always does unexpectedly—excessive risk turns a small correction into a catastrophe for the account.

## The mandatory pause rule

One of the most effective tools for combating euphoria is a mandatory rest. A professional approach involves closing the terminal after reaching a specific profit target for the day, week, or month. If you feel a surge of omnipotence and a desire to immediately open a new trade to double your success, this is the first sign that it is time to take a break. Get away from the monitor for at least 24 hours. Physically distancing yourself from the charts allows dopamine levels to return to normal. It is important to realize that the market is not going anywhere and opportunities to profit will exist tomorrow and a year from now. The main task is to preserve your resources for these opportunities.

## Objective audit of completed trades

To reconnect with reality, you must decompose your success. Open your trading journal and analyze every trade from your winning streak. Were they executed strictly according to your algorithm? Was there an element of luck involved, where the price barely missed the stop-loss before heading into profit? It often turns out that profit was obtained not due to skill, but despite rule violations, simply due to a favorable trend. A thorough post-mortem helps you realize that success can be random. This is sobering and restores discipline. If you see that you deviated from your strategy but still made a profit, this is not a cause for joy, but a cause for serious concern.

## Maintaining risk discipline

The best defense against emotional swings is automation or strict adherence to risk management. Even if you feel like the king of the market, your position size must not exceed your standard risk per trade.
