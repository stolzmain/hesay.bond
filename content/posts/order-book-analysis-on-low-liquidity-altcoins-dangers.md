## Critical Spreads and Instant Losses

One of the main dangers of low-liquidity assets is the wide spread—the difference between the best Ask and the best Bid. During moments of low activity, the spread can reach several percent. This means that a trader is in the red the moment they hit the market buy button. Attempting to analyze the order book under such conditions is complicated by the fact that positions between the main limit orders are virtually non-existent. Any attempt to enter a trade with a significant volume leads to substantial slippage, where the final average execution price is significantly worse than calculated. While analyzing the order book, a novice sees an attractive price but fails to realize that the volume available at that price is negligible, and their order will "pierce" the order book by several levels, instantly ruining the trade's expectancy.

## The Mechanics of Spoofing in Empty Markets

Spoofing—placing large limit orders without the intention of executing them—reaches its peak in low-liquidity altcoins. A large player or market maker places a massive "wall" in the order book to create the illusion of strong support or resistance. Traders, seeing such a barrier, open a position expecting a bounce. However, as soon as the price approaches the order, it instantly disappears or moves further away. In markets with low liquidity, it is much easier to cancel or move a large order because the probability of it being suddenly filled by real counter-volume is minimal. Thus, the order book becomes a shadow theater where "walls" serve only to herd the crowd in a direction convenient for the manipulator, forcing people into traps or liquidating positions at unfavorable prices.

## The Psychology of Order Walls and False Benchmarks

In low-liquidity markets, traders tend to trust large limit orders, often referred to as "walls" or "plates." In classical technical analysis, it is believed that price moves from liquidity to liquidity. But on second and third-tier altcoins, the presence of a large buy wall often does not mean the level is protected. On the contrary, it can be bait for those looking for an entry point with a tight stop-loss below that wall. As soon as enough stop-losses accumulate behind the "visible support," the manipulator pulls the order, and the price collapses into an empty order book zone, triggering a cascade of liquidations. Order book analysis in this case provides a false sense of security that results in a quick loss of capital when liquidity suddenly vanishes.

## Risks of Algorithmic Manipulation

The majority of activity in the order books of low-liquidity coins is generated by trading algorithms. These bots create the appearance of a live market through micro-trades and constant order shifting. For an analyst, this creates "noise" that is extremely difficult to interpret. Algorithms are programmed to react to the appearance of any real order.
