## Passive Income in Digital Assets
Crypto lending allows coin holders to provide their funds to borrowers through specialized platforms or protocols. In return, the lender receives an interest rate that often significantly exceeds the offerings of the traditional banking sector. This is especially relevant for long-term investors (hodlers) whose assets sit idle on cold wallets for years. Instead of simply waiting for price appreciation, coins start to generate a flow of liquidity, which significantly increases the total portfolio yield through compound interest.

## The Mechanics of Crypto Loans and Collateral
The foundation of lender safety in the blockchain environment is over-collateralization. Unlike traditional loans, where a borrower can suffice with proof of income, here they are required to provide collateral that exceeds the loan amount. For example, to borrow 1,000 USDT, a user might need to lock $1,500 worth of ETH in a smart contract. If the value of the collateral falls below a critical threshold (LTV — Loan-to-Value), an automatic liquidation is triggered: the collateral is sold on the market, and the funds are returned to the lender, including accrued interest.

## CeFi vs. DeFi: Choosing a Platform
There are two main paths for deploying capital. Centralized platforms (CeFi), such as major exchanges, offer a user-friendly interface and legal support, but require handing over control of private keys. Decentralized protocols (DeFi), like Aave or Compound, run exclusively on smart contract code. There is no human factor or censorship here, but the risk of technical error or protocol exploits falls entirely on the user. An analyst must always weigh the convenience of a custodial service against the transparency and decentralization of blockchain solutions.

## The Main Dangers for the Lender
The primary systemic risk is extreme volatility. During a sharp market crash, the liquidation system may not trigger instantly due to network congestion or lack of liquidity, which can lead to bad debt on the platform. The second threat is smart contract vulnerability. Even protocols that have undergone multiple audits can contain hidden logic flaws that hackers can exploit. Finally, there is the risk of depegging (losing the fiat peg) for stablecoins, which are most often used as the unit of account for interest payments.

## Strategies to Protect Your Capital
To avoid getting rekt, you must adhere to strict investment discipline. First, broad diversification is mandatory: never keep all your available funds on a single protocol or in a single asset. Second, prioritize stablecoins with verified fiat reserves (for example, USDC) or high-cap algorithmic solutions (DAI). Third, regularly monitor the coverage ratio and liquidity volume in pools. If yields suddenly jump to anomalous levels (above 20-30% APY on stablecoins), it is a direct signal of a critical increase in risk.
