## Psychology versus Mathematical Algorithms

Many traders believe that using chart patterns makes them completely immune to subjectivity. In reality, any chart is merely a projection of the expectations, fears, and greed of millions of market participants. Cognitive biases act as filters that only let through the information our consciousness finds convenient at any given moment. As a result, an analyst often sees not the real market situation, but a desired picture dictated by their current position. This turns the decision-making process into an attempt to force market reality to fit internal biases, which inevitably leads to systematic errors in capital management.

## The Danger of Confirmation Bias

One of the most destructive biases in trading is confirmation bias. Once a trader has formed an opinion on the future direction of a price, they begin to subconsciously ignore signals that contradict this scenario. For example, having decided that an asset will moon, an investor focuses on a bullish oscillator divergence while completely ignoring the breakdown of a key support level. We look for evidence that proves us right instead of looking for facts that disprove our hypothesis. This mechanism makes technical analysis highly vulnerable, transforming it from a forecasting method into a way to justify emotionally driven decisions.

## The Illusion of Patterns in Random Data

The human brain has a unique ability to find structures and meaning where none exist; this phenomenon is called apophenia. In technical analysis, this manifests as looking for complex patterns in common market noise. A trader might see a Head and Shoulders or a Golden Ratio in random price fluctuations caused by one-off news events or low liquidity. The clustering illusion makes us believe that a series of winning trades or a specific sequence of candles guarantees future success. In reality, a significant portion of market movement is chaotic, and attempting to find rigid logic in it often leads to false predictions and unjustified risks.

## The Impact of Emotional Attachment to a Position

The endowment effect and loss aversion cause traders to evaluate charts with bias as soon as they open a trade. From that moment on, the asset becomes "theirs," and the objectivity of the analysis drops sharply. Instead of closing a losing position when a stop-loss is hit, the trader starts moving support levels lower, hoping for an imminent reversal. We tend to overestimate the probability of a favorable outcome simply because we have already invested our time and capital into the idea. This bias turns a disciplined analyst into a gambler hoping for a miracle despite clear technical signals of a trend reversal.
