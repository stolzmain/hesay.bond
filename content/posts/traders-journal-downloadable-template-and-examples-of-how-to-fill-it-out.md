## The Foundation of Systematic Trading

The main goal of keeping a journal is to create a feedback loop. A trader who does not record their actions is doomed to repeat the same mistakes in a circle without noticing their systematic nature. A professional approach involves recording every trade at the moment it is planned. This builds discipline: when you know that every trade must be justified to yourself on paper, the desire to enter the market based on emotions or luck decreases significantly. A journal helps track the expected value of your strategy, the average winning and losing trade, and the maximum drawdown, which is critical for long-term survival in the market.

## What Must Be Included in the Records

A high-quality journal template should contain two data blocks: quantitative and qualitative. Quantitative data includes the date and time of entry/exit, the trading pair, the direction (Long/Short), position size, entry point, stop-loss and take-profit levels, and the final financial result in both currency and percentage of the deposit. However, raw numbers do not give the full picture. The qualitative block includes screenshots of the chart at the moment of opening and closing the trade, a description of the trading setup (e.g., "level breakout" or "bounce off liquidity zone"), and, most importantly, an assessment of the emotional state. Recording your stress level or confidence helps understand how psychology influences the execution of your trading plan.

## Example of a Detailed Trade Analysis

Let's look at a filling example. Instrument: BTC/USDT. Strategy: Test of the 60,000 support level. Entry: 60,200, Stop Loss: 59,700, Take Profit: 62,000. Risk/reward ratio: 1 to 3.6. In the "Comment" field, the trader writes: "Price approached the level on decreasing volume, formed a fakeout with a recovery. Entered with a limit order. Emotions: calm, risk respected." After closing the trade, the result is added: "Closed at take-profit. Price reached the target in 4 hours. Error: moved stop-loss to breakeven too early, nearly got stopped out by market noise." Such analysis allows you to see that the strategy is working, but the position management technique needs adjustment. Compare this to a novice's entry: "Bought BTC because everyone is talking about a pump." The second entry is useless for analysis and provides zero educational value.

## Tools for Keeping Records

The choice of platform depends on your preferences and trading intensity. The classic option is Microsoft Excel or Google Sheets. These are flexible tools that allow you to set up any formulas for automatic calculation of your win rate and equity curve. For those who value visual components and mobility, Notion works great—it is convenient to store screenshots and make text notes there. There are also specialized automated services (Tradervue, MaxProfit, etc.) that connect to the exchange via API and pull trade history automatically. However, even when using automation, manual entry of comments and screenshots remains a mandatory condition for a trader's qualitative growth.
