When a trader spends too much time at the terminal, their attention blunts, and their ability to objectively evaluate market signals diminishes. ## Psychological Traps and Dopamine

The main cause of overtrading is emotional imbalance. Trading is a high-stress environment where every trade triggers a powerful hormonal release. After a series of profitable operations, a trader enters a state of euphoria, feeling like the master of the market. This drives them to open new positions with excessive leverage, ignoring the risks. On the other hand, after losses, the tilt mechanism kicks in—an uncontrollable urge to immediately win back the money. In both cases, the trader is driven by a hormonal cocktail rather than cold calculation. It is important to understand: the market is not a casino, and any attempt to impose one's will on it through high-frequency trading ends in the player's defeat.

## Limits as the Foundation for Survival

To combat overtrading, it is necessary to implement strict quantitative restrictions. A professional approach involves setting limits on the number of trades per day and the maximum drawdown. For instance, if a trading plan restricts activity to three trades per day, a fourth entry should not be considered under any circumstances, even if an ideal signal appears on the chart. A daily stop loss is an even more critical tool. As soon as the total loss for a session reaches 1-2% of the deposit, the trading terminal must be closed. These rules create a safety perimeter that protects capital from the trader's own emotional impulses.

## Algorithming the Trading Process

Chaos is the primary ally of overtrading. To eliminate random entries, every step must be documented in a checklist. If a trading system requires five conditions to align (e.g., support level, oscillator signal, price action pattern, volume, and session timing), the absence of even one of them makes an entry impossible. Using trading alerts allows the trader to avoid staring at the monitor for hours waiting for a signal. This reduces psychological pressure and prevents attempts to force trades where the market is in a phase of uncertainty. A professional trades only what they understand and only when the market grants permission.

## The Mandatory Pause Rule

One of the most effective methods for preserving a deposit is implementing a mandatory break. After closing any trade—whether profitable or losing—a trader needs time for an emotional reset. During this period (ranging from 15 minutes to several hours), opening new positions is prohibited. A pause helps lower cortisol or adrenaline levels, returning the brain to an analytical state. Furthermore, it is beneficial to practice trading-free days, where the market is analyzed but no trades are executed. This helps one realize that having no position is also a position, and often the most profitable one in terms of capital preservation.
