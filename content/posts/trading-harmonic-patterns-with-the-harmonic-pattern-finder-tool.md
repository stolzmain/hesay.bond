## Classification and Structure of Key Models
For successful trading, one must understand the internal logic of the core patterns detected by the tool. The Gartley model is considered a classic and requires the B point to retrace exactly 61.8% of the XA wave, with completion at the D point at the 78.6% level. The Bat pattern is characterized by a deeper test of the 88.6% level at point D, which often misleads inexperienced market participants who mistake it for a breakout. Conversely, the Butterfly and Crab are extension models, where the D point extends beyond the initial X point. For example, in a Crab pattern, the extension can reach an extreme value of 161.8%, signaling that an asset is heavily overbought or oversold. Harmonic Pattern Finder automatically classifies these types, indicating specific target levels for each formation. Understanding these differences is critical, as each pattern has its own specific price behavior within the reversal zone. Entry accuracy in harmonic trading is measured not by pips, but by the percentage of compliance with the ideal mathematical model, which provides an edge over the crowd.

## Determining the Potential Reversal Zone
The Potential Reversal Zone (PRZ) is the foundation of the entire harmonic strategy. This is an area where several Fibonacci levels converge, creating powerful resistance or support. Harmonic Pattern Finder highlights this zone on the chart with color, helping the trader focus attention on a narrow price range.
