## The mechanics of forced position closure

A liquidation occurs when an exchange forcibly closes a trader's position due to insufficient margin to maintain an open order. At this moment, a market order opposite to the direction of the position is thrown onto the market: a long liquidation results in a market sell, while a short liquidation triggers a market buy. When many such events occur, a liquidation cascade arises, pushing the price further and triggering new stop-losses. For smart money, these cascades create ideal conditions to fill large orders with minimal slippage. Entering against the crowd at the moment of liquidation climax is a classic strategy for finding a bottom or a local peak.

## Liquidation maps as a primary reference

One of the most powerful tools on Coinglass is the Liquidation Heatmap. It visualizes the price levels where the highest number of liquidations for traders with various leverage (from 10x to 100x) is concentrated. Visually, this appears as bright clusters or shelves of liquidity. Price often moves toward these zones like a magnet to sweep the accumulated liquidity. A trader should look for moments when the price approaches a large cluster of liquidations, followed by a sharp impulse and subsequent deceleration. If the price does not continue its movement after absorbing a large block of liquidity, it is a sure sign of trend exhaustion and a potential reversal.

## Leverage ratios and concentration levels

Analyzing aggregated 24-hour liquidation data allows for understanding the overall market sentiment. If the volume of liquidated longs significantly exceeds shorts over the last day, it indicates excessive optimism that has been punished by the market. However, for entry purposes, short-term timeframes are more important. On Coinglass charts, you can track cumulative liquidations in real-time. When the liquidation bar chart shows an abnormally high spike, it often coincides with the formation of a shadow (wick) on a Japanese candlestick. This is the moment of maximum pain for retail players, which professionals use to enter in the opposite direction.

## Finding the reversal point after an impulse

The liquidity hunting strategy is based on waiting for a false breakout of a significant support or resistance level. Thousands of stop orders and liquidation zones are often hidden behind these levels. As soon as the price crosses the level, a chain reaction is triggered, creating an instantaneous excess of supply or demand. At this moment, it is necessary to watch the tape and the Coinglass liquidation chart. If we see massive volumes of forced closures, but the price quickly returns back above the level, forming an SFP (Swing Failure Pattern), this is an ideal signal to open a position. In this case, liquidations act as fuel for the reversal move.

## Relationship with open interest

To confirm the validity of a liquidation signal, it is necessary to use Open Interest. If the price falls, massive long liquidations occur, and open interest drops sharply, this confirms that weak hands are exiting the market. Clearing the market of excessive leverage makes the current structure healthier.
