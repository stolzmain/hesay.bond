A six-month preparation plan is the optimal timeframe to transition from chaotic button-mashing to conscious capital management. During this period, it is critical not to chase outsized gains, but to focus on understanding market mechanics and forming trading habits that will allow you to survive in the long run.

## Fundamentals and Market Mechanics
The first month of training should be entirely dedicated to the theoretical foundation. It is impossible to trade profitably without understanding how the exchange ecosystem works. Start by studying basic financial instruments: stocks, currency pairs, futures, and cryptocurrencies. Understand the roles of market participants — from large institutional banks and market makers to retail traders. Master the basic terminology: what an order book, liquidity, spread, swap, and leverage are. Set up your workspace in a trading terminal like TradingView or MetaTrader. Your goal at this stage is to learn how to navigate the exchange interface fluently and understand why price moves under the influence of supply and demand.

## Technical Analysis and Chart Structure
In the second month, the focus shifts to visually reading market information. You will need to master Japanese candlesticks and chart patterns that have been repeating for decades. Instead of relying on dozens of complex indicators, focus on a clean chart. Study the concept of market structure: how to identify uptrends and downtrends through a sequence of higher highs and lower lows. Understand the concept of support and resistance levels, as well as zones of high liquidity. Pay special attention to trading volume — it confirms the validity of price movements. By the end of the month, you should be able to conduct a comprehensive technical analysis of any asset, identifying the priority direction of movement across different timeframes.

## Strategy Development and Risk Management
The third month is time to turn knowledge into a system. Trading is a game of probabilities, not predicting the future. You need to create a detailed trading plan that outlines entry conditions, stop-loss placement, and profit-taking targets. Determine your trading style: intraday trading, scalping, or swing trading. Simultaneously, implement strict risk management. Remember the golden rule: risk per trade should not exceed 1–2% of your capital. Study expected value and the importance of the risk-to-reward ratio, which should be at least 1:3. During this time, perform your first backtesting — checking your strategy against historical data from the past several years.

## Psychology and Systematic Demo Trading
The fourth month is dedicated to practicing discipline on a virtual account. The main mistake beginners make is moving to live trading without testing their emotional state. A demo account is not for making virtual millions, but for honing mechanical actions. You must execute at least 50–100 trades in strict accordance with your plan. Start a trading journal where you record every operation, including screenshots of the chart before and after entry. Pay special attention to your emotions: Fear Of Missing Out (FOMO) or the desire to win back losses (revenge trading). At this stage, you are learning to accept stop-losses as a normal part of business operating expenses that do not destroy your mental state.
