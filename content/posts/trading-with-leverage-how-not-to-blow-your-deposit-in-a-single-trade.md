## Mechanics of Margin Trading and Hidden Threats

The essence of leverage lies in using borrowed funds to open positions that exceed the trader's actual equity. If you use 10x leverage, every 1% move in the asset price brings you a 10% gain or loss on your collateral (margin). The main danger lies in asymmetry: to recover a deposit after a 50% loss, you need to make 100% gains just to break even. With high leverage, market noise that would normally be an insignificant fluctuation turns into a fatal event. Liquidation occurs the moment your position loss equals your collateral. It is crucial to understand that the exchange will never risk its own capital; it will force-close your position as soon as the risk touches your own deposit.

## The Psychology of Greed and the Leverage Effect

The primary reason for blowing up an account in a single trade is not technical flaw in trading systems, but cognitive bias. High leverage creates the illusion of quick wealth, which shuts down the rational planning center in a trader's brain. Under the influence of dopamine from potential super-profits, a player ignores danger signals and overestimates the probability of success. A "do or die" mentality emerges, turning trading into pure gambling. A professional always plans for the worst-case scenario. If you cannot sleep soundly with an open position, your leverage exceeds your psychological risk threshold. Emotional stability is the first barrier to capital preservation.

## Calculating Position Size Correctly

A critical mistake beginners make is selecting leverage in the terminal before calculating the risk per trade. A professional algorithm works differently. First, the entry point and the stop-loss exit point are determined based on technical analysis. Then, the percentage distance from the entry to the stop is calculated. Only then, based on risk management rules (e.g., 1% of the deposit), is the permissible position size calculated. Here, leverage acts only as a technical multiplier to reach the required position size if your own capital is insufficient. If your stop-loss is 5% away from your entry price and you take 20x leverage, your liquidation price will be closer than your stop-loss. This is a mathematical dead end that leads to account liquidation.

## Tools for Protecting Capital from Volatility

Working with leverage is impossible without using protective orders. A stop-loss is not just a recommendation; it is an absolute requirement for survival. In conditions of high volatility, especially in the crypto market, "wicks" occur—sharp price impulses capable of stopping you out in fractions of a second. Using isolated margin instead of cross-margin allows you to limit risk to one specific trade without putting your entire account balance at risk. Experts also recommend using trailing stops to lock in profits, ensuring that a profitable trade does not turn into a loss in the event of a sudden market reversal. Capital protection always comes first; seeking profit comes second.
