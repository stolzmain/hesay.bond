## Digital transformation of public finance

CBDCs represent a digital form of national currency that serves as a direct liability of the central bank. Unlike electronic money held in commercial bank accounts, these assets carry the status of legal tender equivalent to physical cash. This is the regulators' response to the rapid growth of private stablecoins and decentralized finance (DeFi), which are beginning to threaten state monetary sovereignty. Implementing CBDCs allows for combining the convenience of digital payments with the security of government guarantees, creating a new environment for interbank and retail settlements.

## Economic efficiency and settlement speed

For the global economy, the adoption of CBDCs implies a radical reduction in transaction costs. Modern cross-border transfers pass through a complex network of correspondent banks, which is both time-consuming and expensive. Digital currencies allow for instantaneous 24/7 settlements. Furthermore, the transparency of cash flows is increased, which significantly simplifies the fight against money laundering and shadow schemes. For traders and investors, this means an influx of liquidity and the ability to automate complex settlements via smart contracts embedded directly into the national currency protocol.

## Programmability as a monetary policy tool

One of the most innovative features of CBDCs is the concept of programmable money. Central banks gain a tool for surgical liquidity management in the economy. Government subsidies can be programmed to be spent only in specific sectors or within a limited timeframe. This paves the way for implementing the concept of helicopter money with surgical precision. However, for the market, this carries the risk of increased state intervention. The ability to apply negative interest rates directly to citizens' wallets could stimulate consumption but simultaneously limit opportunities for capital accumulation.

## Challenges to privacy and freedom

The main argument of CBDC critics is the threat of total financial surveillance. Unlike cash, every transaction in a digital ruble, yuan, or euro leaves a digital trail. Regulators gain the ability to track citizens' spending in real time. This creates risks of financial censorship, where access to funds could be restricted for administrative reasons. For the professional community, this aspect is the most painful, as it undermines the principle of financial autonomy that was historically ensured by physical cash. The balance between system security and the right to privacy will become the primary dilemma of the coming decade.

## Transformation of the traditional banking sector

Widespread adoption of CBDCs could trigger the disintermediation of the banking sector. If businesses and individuals prefer to hold funds directly in central bank accounts, commercial banks will lose their traditional deposit base. This would reduce their ability to issue loans and could destabilize the conventional two-tier financial system.
