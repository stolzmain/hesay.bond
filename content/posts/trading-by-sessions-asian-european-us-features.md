## Features of the Pacific and Asian sessions

The trading day begins with Sydney and Tokyo. The Asian session is often characterized by moderate volatility and narrower trading ranges compared to Western markets. During this time, institutional players from Japan, China, Australia, and New Zealand are the primary drivers of activity. Major currency pairs like EUR/USD often enter lateral consolidation, making this time ideal for mean reversion strategies or range trading.

However, for pairs involving the Yen (JPY), Australian Dollar (AUD), and New Zealand Dollar (NZD), this is a period of peak activity. It is crucial to note that key economic data from China and Australia are released during these hours, which can trigger sharp impulse moves. Spreads may be slightly wider at this time due to lower liquidity compared to the European period, so scalpers should exercise caution.

## The European breakout and London dominance

With the opening of Frankfurt and London, the market transforms. The London session is the most liquid and volatile, as over 30% of global currency transaction volume flows through the British capital. The daily trend is often established in the early hours of the European session. This is where breakout strategies, specifically the London Breakout, are most effective.

The European session is known for deep corrections and aggressive moves. Traders focus primarily on pairs involving the Euro (EUR), British Pound (GBP), and Swiss Franc (CHF). A high concentration of capital allows large players to move price effectively, creating excellent opportunities for trend trading. However, beginners should beware of "false breakouts," which often occur in the first 30–60 minutes after London opens while the market seeks direction.

## The aggressive volatility of the American session

The New York session brings a new wave of liquidity to the market. This is the time for "smart money" and the release of major US macroeconomic indicators, which set the tone for the entire global market. The American session is marked by high aggression: moves here can be fast and unpredictable. The primary focus shifts to pairs involving the US Dollar (USD), as well as gold and oil.

It is worth noting that American traders often close positions before the weekend or at the end of the trading day, which can lead to sharp reversals of trends formed in Europe. Furthermore, the second half of the American session (after London closes) is characterized by a drop in volume, which sometimes results in sharp price spikes that are not backed by real capital.

## The golden hours of session overlaps

The most significant period for any trader is the "overlap" — the time when the European and American sessions are operating simultaneously (typically from 13:00 to 17:00 UTC). During this four-hour window, liquidity reaches its peak. This is the time of the most powerful directional moves, minimal spreads, and maximum order execution without slippage.
