Each candle in this formation should open within the body of the previous candle (ideally above its midpoint) and close at or very near its low. This demonstrates the resolve of sellers who refuse to let the price bounce back over three trading sessions. ## The Role of Trading Volume in Confirmation

For a professional trader, a chart formation alone is only half the signal. Without volume analysis, the Three Black Crows can turn out to be a trap or a temporary correction. A true reversal is always confirmed by a surge in activity. In an ideal scenario, trading volume should increase with each subsequent candle of the pattern. A rise in volume on the first candle signals the beginning of profit-taking by whales. The second candle on increased volume confirms that the decline is not a random slippage, and on the third candle, volume peaks, indicating panic selling by retail traders and the final capitulation of bulls. If, however, we see three long red candles on decreasing or extremely low volume, it is a reason for caution: there may simply be a lack of liquidity in the market, and the price is falling under its own weight without real selling pressure. In such a case, there is a high probability of a sharp snapback to initial levels.

## Market Participant Psychology

Behind every candle on the chart lies the emotional state of thousands of traders. The first crow is usually perceived by optimists as a healthy correction or an opportunity to buy the dip. However, when the second candle opens higher and is immediately engulfed by new sell orders, closing even lower, anxiety begins to mount among buyers. The psychological break occurs on the third candle. At this moment, those who went long at the peak start closing positions en masse via stop-losses, and market makers pull their buy orders, realizing that the initiative has fully shifted to the bears. The Three Black Crows pattern with volume confirmation is a graphical representation of the moment when the fear of losing capital finally overcomes greed and the hope for continued growth. The longer the candle bodies and the shorter their wicks, the higher the level of panic and the more reliable the sell signal.

## Entry Technique and Stop-Loss Placement

Working with this model requires discipline and strict adherence to entry rules. The aggressive method involves opening a short position immediately after the third candle closes, provided that volume has confirmed the move. A more conservative approach involves waiting for a slight retracement to the midpoint of the third candle's body or to its opening level, which allows for a better risk-reward ratio. The stop-loss is traditionally placed slightly above the high of the first candle in the pattern. If this distance is too large for your risk management, you can use the high of the second candle, although this increases the risk of being stopped out by market noise. Take profit targets are determined based on the nearest strong support levels or by using Fibonacci extensions.
