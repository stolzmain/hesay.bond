Understanding where liquidity is moving gives professional market participants an edge in predicting potential sell-side pressure or, conversely, the formation of a supply deficit. ## Price Suppression via Inflow

The Exchange Inflow metric is traditionally interpreted as a bearish signal. When investors move their coins from cold storage to hot exchange wallets, their primary goal is most often to sell the asset or use it as collateral for margin trading. A spike in inflow, especially if accompanied by an increase in the average transaction size (Exchange Inflow Mean), signals that whales are entering the market. An analyst must consider that a sharp rise in inflow near resistance levels often precedes a correction, as supply on exchange order books increases rapidly, absorbing available demand. However, it is important to distinguish between inflows to spot exchanges and derivative platforms: in the former case, it is a direct sell, while in the latter, it may be a hedge for positions.

## Accumulation and Deficit via Outflow

Exchange Outflow reflects the process of withdrawing assets for long-term storage. This indicator is fundamentally bullish, as it reduces the supply available for immediate sale. When major players move Bitcoin or Ethereum into custodial storage, they create what is known as a supply shock. Under conditions of stable or growing demand, the reduction of liquid reserves on exchanges inevitably leads to price appreciation in the long term. Analyzing outflows helps identify accumulation phases where smart money buys the dip, preparing for a new bull cycle. If an asset's price is stagnant while outflows are consistently growing, it is a classic divergence that indicates buyer strength.

## Calculating Net Flow

To obtain an objective picture, a derivative metric is used: Exchange Net Flow. It is calculated as the difference between inflow and outflow (Inflow minus Outflow). A positive Net Flow value indicates that more assets have entered exchanges than have been withdrawn, which increases the risk of a price drop. A negative value indicates a net outflow and the dominance of a HODL strategy. Professional traders use smoothed moving averages for Net Flow to filter out market noise and identify sustainable trends in the behavior of market participants. Sustained negative Net Flow over several weeks is one of the most reliable signals of a market bottom forming.

## Challenges in Interpreting On-Chain Data

Despite the clear utility, evaluating exchange flows requires a critical approach. One of the main challenges is the internal activity of the exchanges themselves. Transfers between an exchange's cold and hot wallets for liquidity or security purposes can be mistakenly flagged by algorithms as user inflows or outflows. Furthermore, the rising popularity of over-the-counter (OTC) deals allows major players to move massive volumes of assets without directly impacting exchange spot prices in the moment.
