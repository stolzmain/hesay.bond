The fan is based on the mathematical proportions of the golden ratio, which are projected onto the chart as rays originating from a single extreme point.

## Market geometry and Fibonacci numbers

## The mathematical foundation of the fan relies on key coefficients: 38.2%, 50%, and 61.8%. To construct the tool, an analyst first draws an invisible or visible trendline between two significant points—a low and a high (for a bull market) or a high and a low (for a bear market). The software then automatically constructs a vertical segment from the second point to the level of the first. This segment is divided according to Fibonacci proportions, and rays are drawn through these points starting from the beginning of the trend. Thus, each ray acts as a dynamic boundary that shifts along the price scale over time. This allows traders to foresee where price may encounter resistance or support several trading sessions in advance.

## Construction algorithm for an uptrend

## To mark up an ascending chart correctly, it is necessary to identify the start of the impulse, known as a significant local low. This is point A. The second point (B) is placed at the peak of the current trend, where the correction began. By drawing the fan through these coordinates, the trader gets three main rays. The top ray (38.2%) usually serves as the first deceleration zone during a pullback. If the price breaks through it, the main target becomes the 50% level, and the 61.8% ray serves as the strongest support zone. It is important to understand that the slope of the rays depends directly on volatility: the steeper the initial impulse, the faster the support levels will rise in monetary terms. Correctly identifying pivot points requires the skill to filter out market noise, which is why pros often focus on candle wicks on higher timeframes.

## Using the fan in a bear phase

## In a downward move, the logic is inverted. Point A is placed at the peak where the drop began, and point B at the low where the first serious bounce occurred. In this case, the fan rays point downward and act as dynamic resistance levels. In a bear cycle, a breakout of the 61.8% ray to the upside often signals a potential reversal of the global trend or that the market is entering a deep consolidation phase. Traders carefully monitor how price interacts with the rays during corrective rallies: if an asset cannot consolidate above the 38.2% line, it indicates weak buyers and a high probability of retesting local lows.

## Dynamics versus static price levels

## The main advantage of the Fibonacci Fan over standard grids is its adaptability. Horizontal levels are often blown through due to shifts in market context, whereas slanted rays better reflect the psychology of market participants who adjust their expectations based on the speed of price movement. A dynamic level tracks the price. If an asset is moving sideways in a flat, it will inevitably cross the fan rays, signaling that the momentum is fading.
