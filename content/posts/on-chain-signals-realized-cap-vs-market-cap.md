However, in the context of cryptocurrencies, this metric is often deceptive. The main issue is that Market Cap assumes every unit of an asset can be sold at the current price at the exact same moment. In reality, market liquidity is limited, and a significant portion of the supply may be frozen in wallets, lost, or removed from circulation for decades.

Market Cap is extremely sensitive to volatility. A sharp price jump in a thin market can inflate the figure by billions of dollars, even though no proportional volume of actual money has entered the system. This is why experienced analysts view this parameter only as a facade concealing more complex mechanisms of capital distribution and participant sentiment. To get an objective picture, one must look inside the blockchain and evaluate the actual money supply involved in transactions.

## The Mechanics of Realized Capitalization

Realized Cap represents a more accurate and fundamental approach to valuing a network. Unlike Market Cap, it does not use the current exchange price for the entire supply. Instead, the metric values each coin (UTXO) at the price of its last on-chain movement. This makes it possible to exclude the influence of lost coins purchased at pennies in 2011, which are counted at the current rate in Market Cap. Realized Cap is, in essence, the aggregate cost basis of all market participants.

This metric acts as a psychological and economic foundation. It reflects the real volume of fiat currency that has entered the asset and remains in it. If the Realized Cap is growing, it means coins are changing hands at higher prices, and new investors are willing to pay more to existing holders. Stagnation or a decline in this metric signals capital outflows or that coins are moving at lower prices than their previous purchase. Thus, Realized Cap smoothes out market noise, demonstrating the true organic growth or degradation of the network.

## Balance of Power and Market Cycles

The interaction between Market Cap and Realized Cap allows for the determination of market cycle phases with high precision. When an asset's market price soars significantly above its realized value, the market enters a state of overheating. At this moment, the unrealized profit of most participants reaches extreme levels, which historically precedes mass profit-taking and deep corrections. During such periods, the paper value of the asset is not backed by a real influx of liquidity, creating a fragile structure prone to collapse.

The opposite situation is observed during moments of capitulation. When Market Cap falls below the Realized Cap, it means the average investor is underwater. Historically, such periods are zones of maximum opportunity for long-term accumulation. In these moments, weak hands exit the market, selling assets at prices below their average purchase, while smart money absorbs the supply. In this context, Realized Cap acts as a powerful support level, the downward breach of which happens extremely rarely and signals that a global bottom has been reached.
