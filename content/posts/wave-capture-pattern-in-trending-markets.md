## Identifying the Dominant Market Cycle

The Wave Capture pattern begins with identifying a strong impulse. On the chart, this manifests as a series of full-bodied candles confidently breaking through resistance levels (in a bullish trend) or support levels (in a bearish trend). It is crucial for the analyst to ensure the move is not a fakeout. For this, 20 and 50-period Exponential Moving Averages (EMA) are used. If the price is above both lines, and the short-term average is angled upward and positioned above the long-term one, we are dealing with a confirmed uptrend. In this phase, we do not look for entry points; we simply observe the formation of the first impulse wave, waiting for the moment the market begins to breathe — that is, transitioning into a correction.

## Mechanics of the Corrective Pullback

The essence of Wave Capture lies in patiently waiting for a price pullback to the value zone. The value zone is the area between the 20 and 50 EMA or Fibonacci retracement levels (38.2% and 50%). This is where the battle between short-term speculators taking profits and large players accumulating positions for a new move takes place. An important sign of a high-quality pattern is a decrease in trading volume during the pullback. This signals that selling (in the case of an uptrend) is temporary and not supported by smart money. The price should descend smoothly toward dynamic support without breaking the overall structure of higher highs and higher lows.

## Seeking the Entry Trigger

When the price reaches the value zone, a professional looks for a confirmation signal, which is the moment of capture. This could be an engulfing candle pattern, a pin bar, or an inside bar formed directly at the level of the moving average. Within the Wave Capture strategy, it is critical to see buyer reaction: the price must sharply bounce off the level, forming a long lower wick. An additional filter is an oscillator, such as RSI or Stochastic, which should be exiting the oversold zone at that moment. The entry is executed upon the breakout of the signal candle's high, confirming the market's readiness to develop a new impulse wave.

## Risk Management and Targets

The security of the trade is ensured by placing a stop-loss below the local low of the corrective wave. This is a logical level; a break below it would indicate a shift in the market scenario and render the pattern invalid. Profit-taking in the Wave Capture strategy occurs in stages. The first take-profit is recommended at the previous local high, as a temporary pause is possible there. The second part of the position is held until the price reaches the 161.8% Fibonacci extension or until a bearish crossover of the moving averages occurs. This approach allows for maximizing the trade's mathematical expectancy, maintaining a risk-to-reward ratio of at least 1:3.

## Psychology of Systemic Execution

Success in applying the Wave Capture pattern depends not so much on the precision of indicators as on the trader's discipline.
