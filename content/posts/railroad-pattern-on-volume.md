The first candle follows the trend, creating the illusion of continuation, while the second candle almost entirely engulfs the range of the first, returning the price to its starting point. The pattern is named for its resemblance to parallel railroad tracks. However, a chart formation on its own can be deceptive without considering market context. In professional trading, the true power of the Railroads is unlocked only when combined with vertical volume analysis, which acts as a detector for the real intentions of big players. Without liquidity confirmation, the pattern often turns into simple market noise or temporary consolidation.

## Synergy of price and volume

In classic technical analysis, price is considered primary, but for an experienced trader, volume is the fuel that moves the market. When forming the Railroads pattern, volume serves as a verifier. An ideal setup implies that volume remains consistently high on the first candle, confirming the crowd's involvement in the current move. However, the second candle is of critical importance. If its volume is equal to or, preferably, exceeds the volume of the first candle, it signals an aggressive absorption of supply by demand (or vice versa). Increased volume on the second candle indicates that smart money has entered the market at market price, instantly buying up all orders and reversing the price. If the second candle forms on low volume, it suggests a lack of real interest from institutional players, and such a reversal will likely turn out to be a fakeout or a correction.

## Logic of market absorption

From the perspective of market psychology, Railroads on volume represent a classic trap for retail traders. Consider a bearish scenario: the market is in an uptrend, and a powerful bullish candle appears on high volume. At this moment, most breakout traders jump into long positions, fearing FOMO. However, the very next candle opens and plummets, closing at the level where the previous growth began. At that point, all the bulls who opened positions at the highs are trapped. Their stop-losses become fuel for further decline. High volume on the second candle confirms that smart money used the buyers' liquidity to exit their positions or open large shorts. This is a moment of sharp shift in market balance where the dominant force instantly loses initiative.

## Finding the ideal entry point

To trade this pattern effectively, one must wait for the second candle to close completely. Trading inside the bar formation carries high risks, as the price could pull back at the last moment. The optimal entry is executed on the close of the second (reversal) candle or on a slight pullback to its midpoint if volatility was excessively high. It is important that the bodies of both candles are significantly larger than the average size of the previous 10–20 bars. This confirms a true impulse rather than volatility within a flat range. If the pattern forms at significant support or resistance levels, the probability of a successful trade increases exponentially. Railroads appearing in a void (in the middle of a price range) have much lower predictive value, even if they are backed by a volume spike.
