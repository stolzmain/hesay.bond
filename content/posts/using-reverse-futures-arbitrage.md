This strategy becomes particularly relevant when a futures contract trades at an unjustifiably deep discount to the spot price, which often happens during periods of market panic or sharp shifts in market expectations.

The primary objective of the trader is to lock in risk-free profit through price convergence at the contract expiration or when the market spread normalizes. Under conditions of high volatility, futures may trade below the spot price due to excessive selling pressure. Reverse arbitrage allows traders to profit from this anomaly, turning market chaos into a predictable cash flow.

## Technical Aspects of Execution

Implementing this strategy requires impeccable technical preparation. The process begins with monitoring the basis, which is the difference between the spot price and the futures price. When the futures contract trades at a discount exceeding commission costs and the cost of borrowing assets, an entry signal is generated. The trader sells the underlying asset (often through margin lending or borrowing) and simultaneously buys the futures contract.

A key risk here is the funding cost of the short position. If interest rates for borrowing the asset rise too quickly, they can completely erode the arbitrage profit. Therefore, professional traders use algorithmic systems for the instantaneous execution of both legs of the trade, minimizing slippage. Speed is more important than intuition here: a delay of a few milliseconds can turn a profitable trade into a losing one due to a changing spread.

## Risks and Capital Management

Despite its status as a risk-free strategy, reverse arbitrage carries hidden threats. The main risk is liquidity. During sharp market moves, access to borrowing the underlying asset may be restricted, or the cost of borrowing may skyrocket to prohibitive levels. This creates a risk of a margin call on the short position, even if the futures part of the portfolio is profitable.

Additionally, there is counterparty risk if trading is conducted on unregulated venues. Capital management requires strict control over leverage. Professionals aim to minimize the use of borrowed funds to maintain a safety margin in case of sudden volatility spikes. It is also important to account for taxes and transaction costs, which can significantly reduce net returns when trading frequently. Diversification across different assets and exchanges is a prerequisite for the long-term survival of this strategy.

## The Future of Arbitrage Strategies

With the evolution of high-frequency trading and algorithmic platforms, opportunities for manual arbitrage are rapidly shrinking. However, the cryptocurrency market and emerging markets still offer windows of opportunity for those willing to invest in high-quality software and infrastructure. Reverse arbitrage remains one of the most reliable tools for building a portfolio with a fixed yield, independent of market direction.

Success in this field does not depend on predicting price movements, but on the ability to mathematically calculate asset values and manage costs effectively.
