## Structure Essence and Wave Formula

The Expanded Flat is a three-wave structure labeled A-B-C. The main difference between this model and a standard or running flat lies in its extremes. In the expanded version, wave B ends beyond the start of wave A, and wave C travels well beyond the end of wave A. The internal formula of the pattern is 3-3-5. This means waves A and B are corrective threes (zigzags or combinations thereof), while wave C is always a full five-wave impulse or, in rare cases, an ending diagonal triangle. Such a structure indicates high volatility and the emotional instability of market participants at that moment.

## Characteristics of False Breakout Formation

A key characteristic of an expanded flat is the behavior of wave B. When the price begins to move in the direction of the main trend and updates the previous local high (in a bullish cycle) or low (in a bearish cycle), most technical analysts interpret this as the resumption of the impulse. However, within the framework of an expanded flat, this breakout turns out to be false. Wave B merely sweeps liquidity beyond significant levels, after which the market reverses sharply. This happens because the forces of the main trend are not yet strong enough for a full-fledged move, and the market requires a deeper discharge via an aggressive wave C. Traders who entered on the wave B breakout find themselves trapped when the price begins to plummet.

## Impulse Nature of the Final Wave C

Wave C in an expanded flat is the most dynamic and dangerous segment of the pattern. Because it has a five-wave structure, it is often mistaken for the beginning of a powerful new trend in the opposite direction. It does not just reach the end of wave A; it significantly exceeds it. It is at this moment that fear or panic peaks in the market, forcing the last remaining position holders to close at stop-losses. For a wave analyst, the completion of the fifth sub-wave within wave C is an ideal signal to act, as the market usually returns to the main trend with doubled force after the expanded flat ends, striving to compensate for the time spent in the sideways move.

## Key Fibonacci Proportions

To confirm the pattern, professionals use Fibonacci tools. In an expanded flat, wave B most often retraces from 123.6% to 138.2% of the length of wave A. If wave B moves above the 161.8% level, the probability of a flat forming decreases, and the analyst should reconsider the labeling in favor of a different scenario. To forecast the target of wave C, an extension of wave A length is applied. The most common targets are the 161.8% and 261.8% ratios. In rare cases of extreme volatility, wave C can reach 423.6% of the amplitude of wave A. The confluence of these levels with historical support or resistance zones creates a powerful reversal cluster.

## Participant Psychology During Expansion

The Expanded Flat is a reflection of the struggle between greed and fear under conditions of uncertainty.
