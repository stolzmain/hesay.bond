## Instruments for Professional Capital Management

Derivatives are contracts whose value is derived from the price of an underlying asset. Unlike spot trading, where actual coins are transferred between wallets, trading derivatives focuses on shifts in price expectations. This opens up unique opportunities for market participants: profiting from price declines (short positions) and using borrowed funds (leverage) to maximize returns. However, high potential profit always masks symmetrical risks, which require a deep understanding of mathematical models and market psychology.

## Futures Contracts and Their Specifics

Classic crypto futures are obligations to buy or sell an asset at a specific date in the future at a pre-established price. In the crypto market, they are divided into cash-settled and physically delivered contracts. Most traders prefer cash-settled contracts, where the price difference is paid out in stablecoins or the base cryptocurrency without physical asset transfer. A critical aspect here is the gap between the futures price and the spot price, known as the basis. A contango state, where the future is more expensive than the spot, signals bullish sentiment, whereas backwardation points to a prevalence of pessimism. Dated futures are ideal for long-term hedging, allowing miners to lock in selling prices for mined coins or investors to hedge the value of their portfolios.

## The Phenomenon of Perpetual Contracts in Crypto

Perpetual swaps are a unique invention of the crypto industry that has become the most popular instrument among both retail and institutional players. Unlike classic futures, they have no expiration date, which allows for holding a position for as long as desired. The mechanism that keeps the contract price close to the spot price is called the funding rate. If the majority of traders open long positions, they pay a fee to those who opened shorts, and vice versa. This dynamic rate is a key indicator of market overheating. High positive funding often precedes corrections, as the cost of holding longs becomes excessively expensive, triggering position closures and cascading liquidations.

## Strategic Role of Crypto Options

Options represent a more complex, non-linear instrument that gives the holder the right, but not the obligation, to buy (Call) or sell (Put) an asset at a specific price in the future. Unlike futures, where the risk of forced liquidation is always present, an options buyer risks only the premium paid. This makes options an ideal tool for portfolio insurance and creating complex strategies such as straddles or condors, which allow traders to profit from volatility itself, regardless of the direction of price movement. Professional traders closely monitor the Greeks—Delta, Gamma, and Theta—which describe the sensitivity of an option's price to changes in the underlying asset's price, time, and volatility.
