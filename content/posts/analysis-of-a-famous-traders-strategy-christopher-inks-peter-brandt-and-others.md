## The Philosophy of Classical Charting

## Brandt is an adherent of the classical school established by Edwards and Magee. Unlike modern traders who overload their charts with dozens of oscillators, Peter uses virtually naked charts. His main tools are horizontal levels and trendlines that form stable geometric shapes. The core idea is that the market spends most of its time in a state of uncertainty, forming price congestion zones. It is in these consolidation zones that energy accumulates. A trader should not guess where the price will go; their task is to wait for the moment when the market declares its intention through the breakout of a significant pattern boundary. Brandt emphasizes that the most reliable signals are formed on weekly and daily timeframes, as they filter out market noise.

## Geometry as a Reflection of Psychology

## Brandt's arsenal is dominated by simple yet effective patterns: rectangles, triangles, head and shoulders, and multi-week channels. He pays special attention to horizontal boundaries. For him, a rectangle is the ideal model of balance between bulls and bears. The longer the formation of the figure, the more powerful the subsequent move will be. An important detail of the strategy is ignoring false breakouts. Brandt often waits for a candle close beyond the level to confirm the true intention of the market. He views chart patterns not as predictive tools, but as roadmaps pointing to areas with high mathematical expectancy for profit. If a figure lacks clear outlines, he simply stays out of the market, exhibiting phenomenal patience.

## Technique for Identifying Reliable Setups

## The trading process according to Brandt begins with looking for chart purity. If a pattern looks questionable or requires imagination, it is discarded. Peter prefers formations that last from 8 to 26 weeks. This allows for identifying deep structural changes in participant sentiment. Special importance is given to volume: a level breakout must be accompanied by a spike in activity, confirming the interest of big players. However, Brandt warns against over-relying on volume in today's fragmented markets, prioritizing price action above all else. His trade entry is always a stop order for a breakout of a local extreme. He never buys the bottom or sells the top, preferring to capture the main chunk of a confirmed trend.

## Mathematical Superiority and Risk

## Many believe Brandt's success lies in his ability to read charts, but he himself claims the secret is in capital management. His rule is to never risk more than 1% of his capital on a single trade. In doing so, he strives for an asymmetric risk-reward ratio, where potential profit exceeds the potential loss by 3-5 times. Brandt openly states that his winning trade percentage rarely exceeds 40%. His capital grows because rare, large wins cover a series of small, systematic losses.
