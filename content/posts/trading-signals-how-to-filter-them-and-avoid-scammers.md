## The Signal Market: The Illusion of Easy Money

The trading signal industry exploits two major human weaknesses: laziness and greed. Hundreds of channels promising a win rate of over 90 percent appear daily on social media and messaging apps. From the standpoint of mathematical statistics and market randomness, such figures are virtually unattainable over the long run. Most of these services operate on the survivor bias principle: they create dozens of groups, giving opposite forecasts in each. Eventually, one group shows a series of profitable trades by pure coincidence, and it is that group that begins to be aggressively marketed as expert. It is vital for a trader to understand that a signal is not an order to act, but merely the opinion of another market participant, which can be wrong at any moment.

## Signs of Overt Trading Fraud

A malicious actor can be identified by several characteristic traits. First, guarantees of profitability. No professional will offer guarantees in the face of market uncertainty. If you are promised a fixed monthly percentage of profit, you are likely looking at a Ponzi scheme or a scammer. Second, the absence of a history of losing trades. In real trading, losses are inevitable. If the signal feed only contains green reports, the author is either deleting unsuccessful posts or hiding stop losses. Third, insistent suggestions to trade with a specific offshore broker via a referral link. Often, such analysts receive compensation not for your success, but for the draining of your deposit, known as the Revenue Share model in affiliate programs.

## Tools for Independent Performance Verification

The only solid proof of a provider's professionalism is verified monitoring on independent resources such as Myfxbook or MQL5. Screenshots from a terminal or Excel spreadsheets are easily forged in graphic editors. Genuine monitoring allows you to see not only the total profit but also critically important metrics: maximum drawdown, recovery factor, average hold time, and the equity curve. If a signal author refuses to provide a link to public monitoring of their account, citing strategy secrecy, any of their claims regarding profitability should be considered a lie. A professional has nothing to hide, as their statistics are their reputation.

## Technical Audit of Trade Rationale

A high-quality signal always contains three mandatory elements: an entry point, a stop loss level, and a take profit target. Furthermore, a reputable analyst always accompanies the recommendation with a brief rationale. This could be a marked-up chart, a description of a fundamental factor, or a reference to an indicator model. If a signal looks like "Buy Gold NOW," it is a sign of amateurism. Filtering signals should include checking the risk-to-reward ratio. If you are asked to risk 100 points to make 10 points, such a strategy is mathematically doomed to fail. An optimal ratio should start at 1:2 or 1:3 in favor of potential profit.
