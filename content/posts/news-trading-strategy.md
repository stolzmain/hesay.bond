## The Influence of Fundamental Factors on Quotes

The market lives on expectations. In most cases, an asset price already includes the consensus forecast of analysts. The primary move occurs not at the moment the news is released per se, but at the moment of dissonance between the expected value and the actual figures. If the actual data deviates significantly from the forecast, an immediate revaluation of the asset occurs by major institutional players. This creates a price gap or a powerful directional move. It is important for a trader to understand that bad news can trigger growth if it turns out to be less bad than experts expected. It is this difference, known as the data surprise, that serves as the main profit engine in news trading.

## Selecting Key Economic Indicators

Not all news is equally profitable. Experienced analysts focus on red-tier events in the economic calendar. These include central bank interest rate decisions, inflation data (CPI), labor market reports (such as US Non-Farm Payrolls), and Gross Domestic Product (GDP) indicators. Each of these events is capable of driving moves of dozens or hundreds of pips. In addition to scheduled publications, breaking news is of critical importance: geopolitical conflicts, sudden statements from regulators, or natural disasters. Successful trading requires the ability to filter out secondary information and focus on factors affecting liquidity and monetary policy.

## Technical Methods for Executing Trades

There are three primary approaches to news trading. The first is trading before the data release, based on insider information or analytical forecasts. This is the riskiest method. The second is trading at the moment of release (the Straddle Strategy), where Buy Stop and Sell Stop orders are placed above and below the current price a few minutes before publication. The goal is to catch the impulse in either direction. The third method is trading on the pullback or confirmation, where the trader waits for the initial market reaction, the absorption of volatility, and enters a position after the direction of the move has stabilized. The latter option is considered the most conservative and safe, as it allows one to avoid whipsaws — sharp moves in both directions that trigger stop-losses.

## Risk Management Under Turbulence

High volatility carries not only opportunities but also immense risks. One of the main problems is slippage and spread widening. During the release of important data, liquidity can vanish instantly, causing an order to be executed at a price significantly worse than planned. To minimize losses, it is necessary to use strict stop-losses, but setting them too close to the opening price is also dangerous due to market noise. Analysts recommend reducing position sizes by 2-3 times compared to standard intraday trading.
