## Anatomy of a Classic Reversal Model
## The structure of the pattern includes three consecutive market highs. The central peak, called the head, is the highest point in the formation. Flanking it are two lower highs, the shoulders. Under ideal conditions, the left and right shoulders should be at approximately the same price level, though in practice, minor deviations are permissible. The line connecting the two lows formed between the shoulders and the head is called the neckline. This level serves as the fundamental support for the entire construction. As long as the price remains above the neckline, the uptrend is formally intact, but once a breakout occurs from top to bottom, the pattern is considered complete and triggers a signal for active trading.

## The Role and Slope of the Neckline
## The neckline is not always strictly horizontal; it can slope upward or downward. A slope in the direction of the primary trend (upward) is considered a more conservative signal, indicating a gradual weakening of buyers. Conversely, a slope against the trend (downward) indicates aggressive pressure from bears who managed to push the price down even before the right shoulder formed. It is important to remember that until the price closes below this line, the figure remains purely hypothetical. False breakouts frequently occur during periods of low volatility, so experienced analysts always wait for a candle close below the level on a significant timeframe to filter out market noise.

## Psychological Backdrop of Market Cycles
## The formation of the left shoulder occurs during a phase of confident growth when buyer optimism is at its peak. The subsequent pullback and formation of the head show that the market is still capable of hitting new highs, but trading volume at this stage often begins to decline. The most critical signal is provided by the right shoulder: the price attempts to continue the rally but fails to even approach the peak of the head. This is a clear demonstration that demand is exhausted and large players have begun taking profits or opening short positions. The market's failure to break the previous high is the first serious sign that the long-term balance of power has shifted toward the sellers.

## Mechanics of Effective Trade Entry
## The classic trading strategy involves opening a short position at the moment of the neckline breakout. There are two main approaches to executing orders. Aggressive traders enter the market immediately upon the breakout, aiming to capture the entire impulse move. The conservative approach involves waiting for a retest—the moment when the price returns to the neckline after the breakout, tests it as new resistance, and resumes its decline. Special attention should be paid to volume: a true breakout of the neckline is almost always accompanied by a sharp spike in trading activity, which confirms the seriousness of the sellers' intentions and increases the probability of the pattern playing out successfully.
