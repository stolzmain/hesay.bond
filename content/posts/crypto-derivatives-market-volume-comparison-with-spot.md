## Transformation of Investor Trading Priorities

The transition from spot trading to derivatives marked a phase of market maturation. While the spot market implies immediate delivery of an asset and full payment of its cost, derivatives allow for trading contracts based on future value. This shift is driven by the need for more flexible strategies. Institutional players and hedge funds use futures and options not only for speculation but also to hedge their positions against sharp drawdowns. As a result, we are seeing a qualitative change in the structure of participants: retail holders have been replaced by professional traders for whom derivatives are the primary working tool, thanks to their high liquidity and the ability to extract profit from any market movement.

## Statistical Gap in Trading Volumes

When comparing quantitative indicators, the difference between spot and derivatives becomes obvious. According to current analytical reports, the trading volume of derivative instruments (futures, perpetual swaps, and options) consistently exceeds spot market volumes by 3 to 5 times, and during periods of high volatility, this gap can reach tenfold. On major crypto exchanges, daily derivatives turnover is measured in tens of billions of dollars, while spot sections show significantly more modest results. This disparity is explained by the specifics of the crypto industry, where perpetual swaps have become the gold standard of trading, offering users the convenience of traditional futures without the need to track expiration dates.

## Advantages of Derivatives over Spot

The main reason for the popularity of derivatives is capital efficiency. Using leverage allows traders to open positions significantly exceeding their own balance. In the spot market, buying 1 BTC requires the full amount of its market value, whereas in the futures market, it is sufficient to provide only collateral (margin). This creates a massive multiplier for trading turnover. Furthermore, derivatives solve the short-selling problem. While "shorting" an asset on spot is technically difficult and often expensive due to margin lending mechanisms, selling a contract in derivatives is a natural operation, which is critical for effective price discovery and market balancing during bear cycles.

## Impact of Leverage on the Market

High volumes of derivatives also carry specific risks that directly reflect on price behavior. The use of leverage creates a phenomenon of cascading liquidations. When an asset price reaches the level of forced closure for large positions, a chain reaction occurs, triggering sharp dumps or pumps—so-called "squeezes." This is precisely why cryptocurrency charts often show long wicks not backed by fundamental changes in the spot market.
