## Security and Asset Protection

The primary criterion is security architecture. Professional analysis starts by verifying licenses in reputable jurisdictions and reviewing the platform’s history for past breaches. In the modern industry, the Proof of Reserves (PoR) protocol has become a key trust indicator, allowing users to verify that an exchange holds funds in full and does not misappropriate them for operational expenses.

It is equally important to assess internal security tools: 2FA support, hardware key integration, allowlisting for withdrawal addresses, and the existence of an insurance fund for unforeseen circumstances (SAFU). You should also look at storage methods: top-tier exchanges distribute assets between cold (offline) and hot wallets to minimize the risk of mass theft during cyberattacks.

## Liquidity and Order Book Depth

For a trader, liquidity is more important than trading volume, as the latter is often artificially inflated via wash trading. High liquidity ensures that large orders are executed at market price without significant slippage. This parameter should be analyzed via Order Book Depth — the number of buy and sell orders within 1-2% of the current price.

Low liquidity means that even with a correct market forecast, a trader loses a significant portion of profits on the spread when entering and exiting positions. You should also consider the number of available trading pairs. However, there is no need to chase hundreds of shitcoins: having high-quality pairs against stablecoins (USDT, USDC) and fiat currencies is a sign of a mature platform.

## Trading Fee Structure and Costs

Trading economics are built on minimizing transaction costs. Exchanges use the Maker/Taker model, where makers (who provide liquidity) pay less than takers (who consume liquidity at market price). When choosing a platform, examine the VIP tier schedule: exchanges often offer substantial discounts for reaching specific trading volumes or holding the native exchange token (e.g., BNB, KCS).

Beyond trading fees, it is critical to evaluate withdrawal costs. Some platforms set fixed, high fees for withdrawing specific tokens, making small deposits unprofitable. Professionals always factor these costs into their trading plan, opting for low-gas networks like Arbitrum, Optimism, or Solana if the exchange supports them.

## Geographic Access and Legal Status

In an era of global capital controls, the legal aspect is becoming a defining factor. You must clearly understand whether the exchange operates in your country and what levels of KYC (Know Your Customer) are required for full functionality. Exchanges without mandatory verification are gradually becoming a thing of the past under regulatory pressure, so completing KYC today is effectively a guarantee that your funds will not be frozen at the first request of financial authorities.

It is also worth considering the availability of a P2P platform. Under conditions of restricted direct bank transfers, P2P becomes the primary gateway for fiat on-ramping and off-ramping.
