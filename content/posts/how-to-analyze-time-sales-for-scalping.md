## The Essence and Structure of Order Flow

Each line in the tape carries three key parameters: execution time, price, and lot volume. Color coding typically indicates the aggressive side of the trade: green denotes a buy at the Ask price, while red indicates a sell at the Bid price. Analyzing this data allows you to determine which side is currently dominating the market. In scalping, it is crucial not just to see individual prints, but to evaluate the general flow dynamics. A steady prevalence of one color, combined with directional price movement, indicates strong market pressure that will likely lead to further momentum. A professional gaze focuses on sequences of trades that form a dense stream in one direction.

## Execution Dynamics and Speed

Tape speed is a vital parameter for determining phases of market activity. During quiet periods, trades occur infrequently, signaling a lack of interest from major players and low liquidity. However, before a breakout of a key level or during major news events, the tape begins to accelerate. For an experienced trader, the visual pace of new lines appearing serves as a volatility indicator. A sharp acceleration of prints when approaching a resistance level confirms the presence of real momentum. If the tape suddenly freezes at a local peak, it often signals trend exhaustion and the beginning of profit-taking, which serves as an exit signal.

## Filtering Large Market Participants

One of the primary tasks when analyzing Time & Sales is separating market noise from the actions of institutional participants. Small-volume retail trades cannot move the market, so professionals configure filters in their trading terminals to hide small lots. The focus shifts to block trades or series of large prints left by major funds and banks. The appearance of large volume in the tape at a significant price level indicates the defense or attack of a position by a major player. A scalper seeks to join this movement, using smart money capital as a driver for their trade, which significantly increases the expected value of profit.

## Identifying Hidden Resistance and Icebergs

The tape is the best tool for detecting icebergs. These are hidden limit orders whose real volume is many times larger than the visible portion in the order book. When the price hits a certain level and huge buy volumes pass through the tape without a corresponding increase in price, it is a sign of a hidden seller at work. They absorb all market demand, preventing the price from moving higher. A scalper, noticing such an anomaly, can exit a long position on time or consider a counter-trend entry, knowing the current momentum has met an insurmountable obstacle. The ability to spot hidden absorption helps avoid fakeouts and minimizes stop-losses.

## Interaction Between Tape and Order Book

Effective analysis is impossible without synchronizing the tape with Depth of Market (DOM) data. The order book shows potential liquidity and possible support or resistance levels, while the tape shows the actual realization of these plans.
