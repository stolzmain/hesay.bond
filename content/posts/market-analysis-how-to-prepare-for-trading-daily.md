## Global Macroeconomic Context

The first step should always be a deep dive into fundamental data. It is important to identify high-impact events: central bank speeches, inflation data, interest rate decisions, and labor market reports. When such news hits, technical analysis often stops working, and volatility can instantly wipe out even the most conservative stop-losses. An experienced analyst does not try to guess the direction of a news-driven impulse; instead, they decide whether it is appropriate to be in a position at all. If the calendar is packed with high-impact events, the best strategy may be to preserve capital and stay out of the market. Understanding the fundamental background provides the answer to the "why" behind market moves, serving as a necessary filter for technical signals.

## Analyzing Structure on Higher Timeframes

Many make the fatal mistake of starting their workday on five-minute charts. Professional analysis is always built on a top-down principle. Daily and four-hour charts allow one to see the global trend and the zones of interest for large institutional players. At this stage, it is necessary to mark key liquidity levels where orders are concentrated. It is important to determine whether the market is in a phase of accumulation, distribution, or an active trend. If the global structure is bearish, looking for buys on lower timeframes will involve unjustifiably high risks. Identifying the market context allows a trader to trade in harmony with the main money flow, rather than trying to catch reversals in the middle of nowhere.

## Detailing Local Price Zones

After determining the general direction, it is necessary to narrow the focus and mark levels on the chart relevant to the current session. Special attention should be paid to the previous day's highs and lows, as well as the boundaries of the Asian session, which often act as magnets for price. A detailed map of movement is formed here. The trader must pre-write the conditions: under what price behavior at a specific level they will open a trade. Using tools such as Volume Profile or Fibonacci levels helps confirm the significance of chosen zones. Having pre-defined scenarios eliminates the need to make impulsive decisions during moments of sharp market moves, when emotions begin to dominate logic.

## Intermarket Analysis and Seeking Confirmations

Modern financial markets are closely interconnected, and no asset moves in a vacuum. For a forex trader, the Dollar Index (DXY) is the primary leading indicator. For those working with stocks or cryptocurrencies, the performance of the S&P 500 and general risk-on/risk-off sentiment are often the deciding factors. Understanding where smart money is moving helps to confirm or invalidate a local technical signal.
