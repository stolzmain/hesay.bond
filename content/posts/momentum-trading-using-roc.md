The psychology of this method is simple: when an asset shows a sharp move backed by volume, more participants join in, pushing the quotes even further. However, the key challenge lies in distinguishing true momentum from short-term market noise. To this end, professionals use the Rate of Change (ROC) oscillator, which objectively measures the speed of price changes and helps visualize the asset's dynamics.

## Technical Foundation of the ROC Indicator

The ROC (Rate of Change) indicator is a classic oscillator that measures the percentage change of the current price compared to the price a specific number of periods ago. The formula is simple: the current closing price is divided by the closing price n periods ago, one is subtracted from the result, and the number is multiplied by one hundred. The resulting curve oscillates around a central zero line. Unlike many other indicators, ROC has no upper or lower bounds, allowing it to react more flexibly to extreme market movements. Traders typically use a standard period of 14 for medium-term analysis; however, for scalping, settings can be shortened to 9, and for investment purposes, increased to 21 or more.

## Key Trading Signals

The primary signal in the Momentum strategy is the ROC indicator crossing the zero line. When the indicator line crosses zero from bottom to top, it indicates that bullish momentum is building, providing a buy signal. Conversely, crossing the zero mark from top to bottom suggests bear dominance. It is important to understand that ROC shows not just the direction, but the acceleration. If the price is rising but the ROC line begins to decline (while remaining above zero), it is a sure sign that the uptrend is losing strength and consolidation or a reversal may soon occur. Traders use this data to take profits in a timely manner without waiting for a complete shift in market sentiment.

## Identifying Market Divergences

One of the most powerful analytical tools when using ROC is searching for divergences. A divergence occurs when the price chart forms a new high while the ROC indicator shows a lower high (bearish divergence). This indicates that hidden beneath the outward price growth is an exhaustion of buying power. Bullish divergence is the mirror image: the price sets a new low, but ROC shows a higher point, signaling hidden buyer strength. In momentum trading, divergence serves as a warning sign: opening new positions in the direction of the trend at such moments is extremely risky, and it is better to protect existing positions by tightening stop-losses or closing partial volume.

## Filtering False Positives

Trading exclusively on ROC signals can be dangerous during flat periods, when the price fluctuates within a narrow range. In such conditions, the indicator will frequently cross the zero line, creating numerous false entries. To increase efficiency, professionals combine ROC with trend indicators, such as an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) with a period of 50 or 200. The rule is simple: only consider buy signals from ROC when the price is above the EMA, and sell signals when it is below. Confirmation from trading volumes is also helpful.
