Unlike standard trading volume, which tracks every transaction and resets at the end of each trading period, Open Interest reflects the real sentiment of capital and its willingness to hold positions over an extended horizon. Understanding the dynamics of this indicator in correlation with price action allows traders to identify when a trend is backed by an inflow of fresh liquidity and when it is merely sustained by momentum or the forced liquidation of weak hands.

## Mechanics of Market Signal Formation
## To effectively use the divergence strategy, one must understand the internal logic of the indicator's fluctuations. When Open Interest rises alongside the price, we are witnessing a healthy bullish trend: new buyers are entering the game, creating a foundation for further growth. However, the true value for a trader lies in identifying anomalies. Divergence occurs when the price chart continues moving in its established direction, but the number of active contracts begins to stagnate or sharply decline. This is a clear signal that the fuel in the engine of the current trend is running out and the market is bracing for a major correction or a complete paradigm shift.

## Weakening Upward Momentum
## The most dangerous yet profitable situation for analysis is a price increase occurring against a backdrop of falling Open Interest. In terms of market psychology, this pattern indicates that the rally is not being fueled by aggressive new buying, but rather by the closing of existing short positions. This process is often referred to as a short squeeze or the forced covering of losses by bears. Once sellers finish buying back to close their positions, the additional demand evaporates instantly. Since new buyers are reluctant to enter the market at local highs, the asset loses support and becomes highly vulnerable to a sharp drop. For an experienced strategist, this divergence is a critical signal to take profit on longs or to prepare for opening a short position.

## Signs of a Bearish Cycle Exhaustion
## The inverse situation occurs in a falling market, where quotes continue to hit new lows while Open Interest noticeably declines. This is a classic sign of seller capitulation. Market participants are closing their positions en masse, taking profits on shorts or exiting longs via stop-loss orders. In this phase, there are virtually no new sellers willing to bet on further downside. The energy of the drop is exhausted, creating what is known as an empty fall. As soon as the process of settling old contracts is complete, a liquidity vacuum emerges, which is often filled by a sharp impulse reversal to the upside upon the arrival of even minor buy-side demand.

## Tools to Confirm Trading Hypotheses
## Using Open Interest divergence in isolation can be risky, which is why experts always look for additional filters. One such tool is Cumulative Delta. If the price is rising, OI is falling, and the delta remains negative, it confirms that the growth is driven by market buys to cover shorts rather than strategic position building by whales. It is also critical to monitor liquidity levels. A true reversal via divergence often occurs after a false breakout of a significant support or resistance level, where the maximum amount of retail trader stop orders are concentrated.
