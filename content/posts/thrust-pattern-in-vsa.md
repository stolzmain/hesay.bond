## Key Characteristics of the Price Bar

Identifying a true pattern requires an analysis of three critical components: spread, volume, and closing position. The spread (the range from the bar high to the bar low) must be significantly wider than the average value of the previous 20 to 30 periods. This indicates high volatility and the decisiveness of buyers or sellers. Meanwhile, volume must be abnormally high or, at the very least, substantially exceed average levels. In the VSA system, high volume on a wide spread confirms that the movement is backed by real capital rather than being a result of low liquidity. The third important aspect is the closing price: for a bullish Thrust, it must be in the top third of the bar; for a bearish one, in the bottom third. Closing in the middle or against the main movement signals hidden selling or buying, which calls the validity of the pattern into question.

## The Role of Context and Market Phase

In VSA, context is always more important than an individual bar. The Thrust pattern gains maximum predictive value when it arises after an Accumulation or Distribution phase. If the price has been in a narrow trading range for a long time and suddenly demonstrates a powerful Thrust on high volume, it means that the supply within the range has been fully absorbed and the market is ready for a trend. It is important to consider exactly where the breakout occurred. If the pattern occurs at the peak of a mature trend, it may transform into a Buying Climax, which portends an imminent reversal. Therefore, a trader must always correlate the current momentum with the chart history, assessing whether this bar is the beginning of a new move or the culmination of an old one.

## Trading Signal Confirmation Technique

Despite the visual power of the pattern, entering a trade directly at the close of the Thrust bar carries risks, as the market often makes a short-term pullback to test the broken level. The optimal tactic is to wait for the next bar. If the subsequent candle has a narrow spread and low volume (a No Supply or No Demand signal), this confirms the absence of resistance from the opposing force and clears the way for a safe entry. In classic VSA, a true Thrust should not be immediately retraced by an opposing movement. If the price quickly returns into the body of the Thrust bar and closes below its midpoint, we are dealing with a fakeout. A stop-loss is traditionally placed beyond the extreme of the Thrust bar itself, as price breaking through it completely invalidates the logic of the pattern.

## Participant Psychology and Market Traps

The psychological aspect of the Thrust pattern is built on provoking emotions in the crowd. A fast and strong price impulse triggers a fear of missing out (FOMO) in retail participants, forcing them to open positions while chasing the market.
