## Signal Types in the Trading Terminal

A custom-developed indicator operates using two main types of signals: regular and hidden divergence. Regular divergence occurs when price forms a higher high while the oscillator forms a lower high, foreshadowing a bearish reversal. Conversely, a lower price low paired with a higher oscillator low indicates a bullish reversal. Hidden divergence, in turn, acts as a powerful trend continuation signal. In a bespoke solution, the algorithm is programmed to instantly identify these patterns, marking them on the chart with arrows or trendlines. This frees the trader from the need to manually compare peaks and troughs, minimizing human error and fatigue.

## Advantages of a Custom Indicator

The main difference between a custom indicator and out-of-the-box versions is the flexibility to adjust settings for specific assets and timeframes. Custom development allows for the integration of multiple oscillators into a single tool, such as RSI, MACD, Stochastic, or CCI. The algorithm can analyze data from multiple periods simultaneously, confirming a signal on a higher timeframe with data from a lower one. Furthermore, professional-grade code eliminates the repaint issue often found in free scripts. The trader receives real-time notifications via smartphone push alerts or Telegram messages, ensuring mobility and helping them never miss major market opportunities.

## Technical Parameters and Noise Filtering

One of the critical problems when trading divergence is market noise on smaller time intervals. An indicator built according to custom technical specifications includes advanced filters. This can include filtering by volume, volatility (e.g., using ATR), or confirmation via support and resistance levels. Traders can independently set sensitivity parameters, determining how many candles the analysis should cover when hunting for discrepancies. This approach allows for filtering out weak signals during periods of low liquidity or flat markets, focusing only on patterns with a high probability of follow-through.
