## Fundamentals of order book operation

The Depth of Market is a table of limit orders for buying (Bids) and selling (Asks) for a specific asset. Each price level in the book shows the cumulative volume of contracts or lots that traders are willing to execute at that moment. The difference between the best bid price and the best ask price forms the spread. For a scalper, the order book is not just a list of numbers, but a dynamic liquidity map. When we see a prevalence of large orders on the Bid side, it signals potential price support. Conversely, dense clusters on the Ask side indicate strong resistance. However, it is important to understand that the DOM only displays limit orders. Market orders, which move the price by executing against these limits, are not visible in the DOM until the trade is completed, which requires the trader to simultaneously monitor the tape of prints.

## Analysis of liquidity and order density

The key task for a scalper when working with the Depth of Market indicator is to search for anomalous volumes. These are orders that significantly exceed the average lot size on a given timeframe. Such volumes often belong to institutional players or market makers. Detecting large density allows a scalper to build a strategy with a tight stop-loss: the entry is made in anticipation of a bounce from a large limit level. If the price begins to aggressively eat through this volume, it becomes a signal to enter a breakout trade. It is important to distinguish between real and fake liquidity. Real liquidity is often backed by actual interest in the asset, while rapidly disappearing and reappearing orders can be a sign of algorithms trying to manipulate the crowd's expectations.

## Strategies for rapid trade execution

Using the DOM allows scalpers to employ specific tactics, such as front-running (placing an order in front of a large volume) or trading on absorption momentum. In conditions of high volatility, the order book helps to assess the speed of changes in the mood of the tape. For example, if supply levels in the DOM rapidly thin out with minimal buy volume, it indicates a lack of sellers and the likelihood of a quick impulse move. A scalper enters a trade based on the supply-demand imbalance. The DOM is also indispensable for determining exit points. Seeing a wall of limit orders building up in front of the price, a trader can close a position with minimal profit without waiting for a chart reversal, which is critical for maintaining the positive expected value of a strategy.

## Configuring order volume filtering

To work effectively with the Depth of Market, it is necessary to properly configure your trading terminal. Most professional platforms allow you to filter orders by volume, highlighting the most significant levels with color. This relieves the trader of market noise and allows them to focus on the actions of big players. Modern modifications of the DOM also include Heatmap elements, which visualize the history of changes in limit orders.
